The Post-Trainer's Guide for Multilingual Synthetic Data Generation

By generating over 1.4M synthetic examples from 10 teacher models, and training over 240 student models, we study the multilingual synthetic data pipeline and distill it into a recipe.

Lester James V. Miranda, Ivan Vulić, Anna Korhonen
Language Technology Lab, University of Cambridge
Correspondence to ljvm2@cam.ac.uk

Paper Code HuggingFace

High-quality training data is often scarce for less-resourced languages, and collecting it from native speakers demands substantial human effort, creating a bottleneck for language-specific model development. To alleviate this challenge, synthetic data generation has gained traction as a promising solution: a typically larger teacher model generates training examples, which are then used to finetune a smaller student model to replicate the knowledge of the teacher.

In this work, we study the key design decisions in the synthetic data pipeline: which teacher model to use, which data generation method to apply, and how these choices play out across languages. These decisions are often made ad hoc—teacher selection, for example, typically defaults to the largest available option, even though such models may have significant capability gaps in non-English languages. Based on our findings, we recommend a recipe for generating multilingual synthetic data.
